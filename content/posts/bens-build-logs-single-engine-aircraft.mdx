Hey, Ben the intern here! 👋

I’m an aeronautical engineering student and recently joined Zoo to explore what practical engineering looks like in Zoo Design Studio.

Over the next few months, I’ll be designing a small single-engine aircraft piece by piece and sharing the process along the way.

Excited to see where this ends up ✈️

## Ben’s Build Log #01

While creating the aircraft tires, I built the outer tire cross-section manually to define the geometry.

Instead of recreating essentially the same shape again for the inner profile, I handed that part to Zookeeper. One prompt later, it reproduced the profile with a consistent offset.

![Aircraft tire cross-section and offset inner profile](/documentation-assets/bens-build-logs-single-engine-aircraft/build-log-01-1.jpg)

[View original post on X](https://x.com/zoodotdev/status/2087606795024134441).

## Ben’s Build Log #02

For the wheel rims, I needed some info on how the bolt assembly is typically constructed on aircraft wheels.

I asked Zookeeper for some technical context and it gave me a breakdown and technical drawings to support my modelling.

![Aircraft wheel rim and bolt assembly](/documentation-assets/bens-build-logs-single-engine-aircraft/build-log-02-1.jpg)

[View original post on X](https://x.com/zoodotdev/status/2089428557760274668).

## Ben’s Build Log #03

I was modeling the fork and oleo strut of the nose landing gear when I wondered if Zookeeper could take over from there.

It completed the assembly and even suggested the next steps.

![Nose landing gear fork and oleo strut assembly](/documentation-assets/bens-build-logs-single-engine-aircraft/build-log-03-1.jpg)

[View original post on X](https://x.com/zoodotdev/status/2090859882409082988).

## Ben’s Build Log #04

To model the aircraft wing, I first needed to define the rib and its spar geometry.

I sketched out the rough profiles I wanted by hand and gave them to Zookeeper to size them properly.

![Aircraft wing rib and spar profiles](/documentation-assets/bens-build-logs-single-engine-aircraft/build-log-04-1.jpg)

[View original post on X](https://x.com/zoodotdev/status/2092323099396219246).

## Ben’s Build Log #05

A wing obviously has more than just one rib, so I let Zookeeper duplicate the rib along the entire wingspan.

Then I had him extend the spars and stringers through all the ribs to complete the internal wing structure.

![Ribs, spars, and stringers along the wingspan](/documentation-assets/bens-build-logs-single-engine-aircraft/build-log-05-1.jpg)

[View original post on X](https://x.com/zoodotdev/status/2095572461115134329).

## Ben’s Build Log #06

Before finishing the wings, I wanted to start on the aircraft’s piston engine, beginning with the crankshaft.

I noticed I had gotten the propeller mounting flange slightly wrong, so I gave Zookeeper reference images of a real aircraft crankshaft.

Zookeeper corrected the geometry and laid the groundwork for the rest of the crankshaft.

![Aircraft engine crankshaft and propeller mounting flange](/documentation-assets/bens-build-logs-single-engine-aircraft/build-log-06-1.jpg)

[View original post on X](https://x.com/zoodotdev/status/2097774389072924799).

## Ben’s Build Log #07

After modeling the crankshaft and pistons, I asked Zookeeper to bring everything together.

Instead of positioning every part myself, he took over and assembled the almost complete engine internals.

![Assembled crankshaft, pistons, and engine internals](/documentation-assets/bens-build-logs-single-engine-aircraft/build-log-07-1.jpg)

[View original post on X](https://x.com/zoodotdev/status/2102446183117688953).

## Ben’s Build Log #08

For the valve mechanism, I needed to add compression springs, but wasn’t sure about the best way to model them in Zoo Design Studio.

So I asked Zookeeper which approach he would recommend. He suggested using a helix, which turned out to be exactly what I needed for the spring geometry.

![Compression springs in the engine valve mechanism](/documentation-assets/bens-build-logs-single-engine-aircraft/build-log-08-1.jpg)

[View original post on X](https://x.com/zoodotdev/status/2103521173867041231).

## Ben’s Build Log #09

To finish up the wing structure, I first refined the stringers to make them more representative of a real aircraft.

On top of that, I modelled two different spar angle designs and gave Zookeeper the placement logic for each of them.

He then positioned and duplicated all 45 angles across the wing structure.

![Wing structure with stringers and spar angles](/documentation-assets/bens-build-logs-single-engine-aircraft/build-log-09-1.jpg)

[View original post on X](https://x.com/zoodotdev/status/2104645609588469944).

## Ben’s Build Log #10

Of course, the wing still needed a proper wingtip, so I gave Zookeeper the existing wing geometry and some basic design requirements while leaving the actual shape mostly up to him.

The first result looked promising, but wasn’t quite there yet. With a few more prompts, I refined the geometry and ended up with a final version that felt much more convincing.

![Refined wingtip geometry](/documentation-assets/bens-build-logs-single-engine-aircraft/build-log-10-1.jpg)

[View original post on X](https://x.com/zoodotdev/status/2105726703607906405).

## Ben’s Build Log #11

In the Zoo Aquarium, I came across a different approach to generating a wing’s NACA profile, so I asked Zookeeper to compare it with the method I was already using.

Turns out my approach was better suited for this model, so I kept it and completed the wing assembly with the final additions of the ailerons and outer skin.

![Completed wing assembly with ailerons and outer skin](/documentation-assets/bens-build-logs-single-engine-aircraft/build-log-11-1.jpg)

[View original post on X](https://x.com/zoodotdev/status/2107173142934532238).

## Ben’s Build Log #12

An important part of the crankshaft and camshaft setup is the timing connection that keeps both shafts synchronized.

In a four-stroke engine the camshaft rotates at half the crankshaft speed, so I had Zookeeper create a 2:1 gear setup between them.

![Crankshaft and camshaft timing gears](/documentation-assets/bens-build-logs-single-engine-aircraft/build-log-12-1.jpg)

[View original post on X](https://x.com/zoodotdev/status/2108327716177187013).

## Ben’s Build Log #13

While reviewing the engine assembly, I realized there was a deeper logic issue in the way I had arranged the layout.

After working through the problem with Zookeeper and exploring a few possible fixes, I reworked the affected geometry to better match the flat-four configuration typical for this kind of aircraft engine.

The result is a more mechanically plausible setup that I’m much happier to continue with.

![Reworked flat-four aircraft engine layout](/documentation-assets/bens-build-logs-single-engine-aircraft/build-log-13-1.jpg)

[View original post on X](https://x.com/zoodotdev/status/2110072699930190229).

## Ben’s Build Log #14

With the rest of the engine components in place, I asked Zookeeper to create a functional linkage between the camshaft lobes and the valves.

The first result already had the basic mechanism in place, but some of the geometry still needed refinement.

After a few adjustments to make the setup cleaner and more realistic, the engine internals were finally complete.

## Ben’s Build Log #16

With KCL 3.0 released, I asked Zookeeper to migrate the entire project, review the existing code and make sure everything still worked as intended.

With that done, I finished the propeller spacer, making use of the improved fillet capabilities in KCL 3.0 to smooth out the transitions around the mounting bolts and structural ribs.

![Propeller spacer with fillets around mounting bolts and ribs](/documentation-assets/bens-build-logs-single-engine-aircraft/build-log-16-1.jpg)

## Ben’s Build Log #17

Before modeling the propeller itself, I asked Zookeeper to evaluate what kind of fixed-pitch propeller would make sense for this type of aircraft and to work out suitable dimensions and blade profiles.

He then used that information to create a series of sketches, visualizing the blade shape, twist and overall proportions.
