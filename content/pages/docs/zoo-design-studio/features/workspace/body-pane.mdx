Bodies appear directly in the [Feature Tree](/docs/zoo-design-studio/features/workspace/feature-tree).
Each body has a body icon and a disclosure arrow. Expand it to see its contributing sketches,
regions, creation features and later modifications in execution order. Collapsing a branch hides
its rows without changing geometry visibility.

Imported parts, bodies and feature groups start collapsed. Expansion choices stay in place during
sketch editing and model execution in the current file. Bodies share one scrolling tree with their features.

## What You Can Do

- Select a body by clicking its row. The selection stays synchronized with the Scene and the
  associated KCL.
- Hide or show a body with the eye control on its row.
- Right-click a body and choose **Delete** to remove it from the model.
- Expand an imported part to inspect its bodies, then expand a body to inspect its features.
- Inspect 3D patterns and edits to their instances within the source body's history.

## Control Body Visibility

Hover over a body row to reveal its eye control. Click the control to hide the body, then click it
again to show the body.

Visibility changes update the model's KCL with a `hide()` operation. A hidden body remains part of
the design and can still be restored from its body row; hiding it does not delete its geometry.

## Delete a Body

Right-click a body row and choose **Delete**. This immediately adds a Delete feature to the model
history and removes that body from the Scene and the Feature Tree. Undo the operation, or remove the
Delete feature, to restore the body.

See [Delete](/docs/zoo-design-studio/features/3d-design/parametric-modeling/transforms/delete) for deleting multiple bodies and the differences between deleting, hiding, and deleting a feature.

## How the List Is Organized

Bodies are labeled `Body 1`, `Body 2`, and so on in display order. Consumed inputs and their
features appear in the resulting body's history. A shared feature can appear under several bodies;
these rows reference the same feature, so editing one updates the shared model.

In assemblies, imported parts contain their bodies and contributing features. Nested subassemblies
retain their hierarchy. Bodies authored in the assembly remain at assembly scope. Parameters and
features without a resolved body association remain at their file scope.

3D pattern copies do not add separate body rows. Body-row selection and visibility apply to the
source body; individual copies remain selectable in the Scene.

## What You Cannot Do

- Rename, reorder, or group bodies in the tree.
- Treat visibility as deletion. Hidden bodies remain in the model and in its KCL.

## Key Differences in This Area

- Bodies and their contributing features share one tree.
- Selecting a body works at the geometry level while remaining linked to its source code.
- Visibility is parametric model state recorded in KCL, not a temporary viewport-only setting.
