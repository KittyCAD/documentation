Bodies appear directly in the [Feature Tree](/docs/zoo-design-studio/features/workspace/feature-tree).
Each body has a body icon and a disclosure arrow. Expand a body to inspect the features that
contribute to it, or collapse it to keep the tree compact.

## What You Can Do

- Select a body by clicking its row. The selection stays synchronized with the Scene and the
  associated KCL.
- Hide or show a body with the eye control on its row.
- Right-click a body and choose **Delete** to remove it from the model.
- Inspect 3D pattern features under their source body without adding a body row for every copy.
- Expand or collapse a body's feature history independently. Collapsing a body does not hide its geometry.

## Control Body Visibility

Hover over a body row to reveal its eye control. Click the control to hide the body, then click it
again to show the body.

Visibility changes update the model's KCL with a `hide()` operation. A hidden body remains part of
the design and can still be restored from its body row; hiding it does not delete its geometry.

## Delete a Body

Right-click a body row and choose **Delete**. This immediately adds a Remove feature to the model
history and removes that body from the Scene and the Feature Tree. Undo the operation, or remove the
Remove feature, to restore the body.

See [Delete](/docs/zoo-design-studio/features/3d-design/parametric-modeling/transforms/delete) for deleting multiple bodies and the differences between deleting, hiding, and deleting a feature.

## How the List Is Organized

Bodies are labeled `Body 1`, `Body 2`, and so on in display order. Their contributing
features are nested below them in execution order and keep their existing editing and source
navigation actions.

A feature shared by several bodies can appear under each of them. These entries refer to the
same feature: editing one updates the shared model. Parameters and features that are not
associated with a body remain at the root of the tree.

3D patterns stay within the source body branch. The pattern feature and later edits to its copies
appear in that body's feature history. Body-row selection and visibility act on the source body;
select individual pattern copies in the Scene.

## What You Cannot Do

- Rename, reorder, or group bodies in the tree.
- Treat visibility as deletion. Hidden bodies remain in the model and in its KCL.

## Key Differences in This Area

- Bodies and their contributing features share one tree and one scrolling area.
- Selecting a body works at the geometry level while remaining linked to its source code.
- Visibility is parametric model state recorded in KCL, not a temporary viewport-only setting.
